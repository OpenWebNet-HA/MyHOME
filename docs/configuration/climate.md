# Climate & Heating (WHO = 4)

The **MyHOME** integration provides full control for heating, cooling, central thermoregulation units, and zone thermostats operating on OpenWebNet **WHO = 4**.

In v2, setup and management are **100% UI-first**: climate zones and central units are automatically discovered over the SCS bus without manual YAML configuration files.

---

## 🚀 Auto-Discovery

When your gateway connects to Home Assistant:

1. **Dynamic Bus Discovery**: The integration listens to OpenWebNet `WHO = 4` temperature and setpoint messages broadcast on the SCS bus.
2. **Entity Creation**: Each active thermoregulation zone (`WHERE = 1..99` or central unit `#0`) is registered as a Home Assistant `climate` entity linked to your gateway.
3. **UI Customization**: You can rename the zone, assign it to an Area (e.g. *Master Bedroom*, *Living Room*), and adjust temperature step increments directly in the Home Assistant UI.

---

## 🏛️ Supported Plant Architectures

MyHOME installations typically deploy one of three climate topologies, all natively supported by the v2 discovery engine:

### 1. 99-Zone Central Unit Architecture (`BTicino 3550` / `#0`)
* **Hardware**: Central thermoregulation unit (BTicino `3550`, MyHOME_Screen 10, or MyHOME_Screen 3.5) operating at master address `WHERE = #0`.
* **Subordinate Zones**: Individual room zones (`WHERE = 1..99`) with local probes (`L4693`, `H4693`, `LN4693`, etc.) and `F430/2` or `F430/4` flush/DIN actuators.
* **Operation**: The integration discovers the central unit (`#0`) as the master `climate` entity and each individual zone (`1..99`) as a controllable `climate` device. Subordinate zone setpoint and mode commands use the central-unit-routed `#Z` address format (`*#4*#Z*#14*TTTT*M##`, `*4*311*#Z##`, `*4*303*#Z##`).
* **Central unit modes**: `off` sends `*4*303*#0##`; `auto` asks the central unit for its program and `heat` / `cool` for the season (OWNd's `set_central_mode` builds the frames). With OWNd 2.0.0b13 `auto` is `*4*311*#0##`, which a live `3550` behind an F461 did not act on (ACKed and forwarded, central unit still OFF 8 s later, 2026-11-12); the OWNd change proposed with this (`set_central_mode('#0', 'auto')` -> the published last-weekly-program command `*4*3100*#0##`, WHO 4 PDF §1.6.8) takes effect once the pin moves to a release that carries it; the `3550` answered that frame within 0.15 s with `*4*2101*#0##` (weekly program 1, conditioning). `off` / `auto` are shown at once and then follow the central unit's report (after a program restore the entity shows the reported season and `program`); `heat` / `cool` wait for the central unit's answer (the mode it is in, or nothing when the season does not change; live F461 + F455, 2026-11-08).
* **Startup & Reconnect Status Burst (`*#4*#0##`)**: On startup (including on SCS-only gateways such as `MH200N` and `F454`), the central unit entity queries `*#4*#0##`. The `3550` responds with its operating mode frame (e.g. `*4*1101*#0##` for Heating Weekly Program 1, `*4*110#0200*#0##` for Manual 20.0 °C, or `*4*102*#0##` for Antifreeze/Protection) followed by diagnostic status frames (`WHAT 20..24, 30, 31`), exposed on the `#0` entity as state attributes:
    - `remote_control`: `"enabled"` (`*4*21*#0##`) or `"disabled"` (`*4*20*#0##`, raises the self-clearing `central_remote_control_disabled` repair issue).
    - `at_least_one_probe_off`: `true` when `*4*22*#0##` is reported in the current burst.
    - `at_least_one_probe_antifreeze`: `true` when `*4*23*#0##` is reported in the current burst.
    - `at_least_one_probe_manual`: `true` when `*4*24*#0##` is reported in the current burst.
    - `failure_detected`: `true` when `*4*30*#0##` (`Failure discovered in a zone`) is reported in the current burst.
    - `battery_fault`: `true` when `*4*31*#0##` (`Central unit battery KO`) is reported in the current burst (raises the self-clearing `central_battery_fault` repair issue).
* **Remote Control (*Telegestione*) Requirement**: On a physical BTicino `3550`, **Remote Control (*Telegestione*)** must be set to **Enabled (*Abilitata*)** in the `3550` menu (`*4*21*#0##`) for the central unit to accept remote commands over the bus. When `*4*20*#0##` is active, the gateway still replies `*#*1##` (ACK), but the `3550` ignores remote mode and setpoint writes.
* **Subordinate Zone Control Patterns (`Auto` vs `Manual`)**:

| Control Pattern | Central Unit (`#0`) Mode | Subordinate Zone (`1..99`) Mode | How Setpoint & Schedule Changes Work |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **1. `3550` as Schedule Master + HA Temporary Overrides** | `auto` (Weekly Program `1..3` or Scenario `1..16`) | `auto` (`*4*111*#Z##` / `*4*211*#Z##`) | Calling `climate.set_temperature` on a zone writes `*#4*#Z*#14*TTTT*3##`, placing that zone into **manual setpoint override** (`*4*110*#Z##`, `hvac_mode: heat`/`cool`, `#0` reports `at_least_one_probe_manual: true`). Setting the zone's `hvac_mode` back to `auto` sends `*4*311*#Z##` and returns the zone to the `3550`'s active weekly schedule. |
| **2. Home Assistant as Full Schedule Master** | `heat`, `cool`, or `auto` | `heat` / `cool` (Manual setpoint per room) | Each zone is driven directly by Home Assistant automations or the HA [Schedule](https://www.home-assistant.io/integrations/schedule/) integration calling `climate.set_temperature` (`*#4*#Z*#14*TTTT*3##`) or `climate.set_hvac_mode` (`heat`, `cool`, `off`) on each room entity independently. |

### 2. 4-Zone Central Unit Architecture
* **Hardware**: BTicino `HC4695`, `L4695`, or `LN4695` 4-zone master display panel.
* **Operation**: The master panel acts as Zone 1 (or its assigned zone number) while coordinating up to three subordinate zones.

### 3. Standalone Probes (No Central Unit)
* **Hardware**: Autonomous room thermostats (BTicino `H4691`, `LN4691`, `L4692`) acting as independent temperature controllers without a master central display.
* **Operation**: Each thermostat is exposed directly as an independent `climate` entity with full target setpoint and mode control.
* **Next to a central unit**: a plant with a central unit can have a thermostat the central unit is not running. On the live test plant a `3550` wrote and reported zones 2, 3 and 4 while it was on its weekly program in conditioning (`*4*211*#2##`, 2026-11-12), and only zones 3 and 4 while it was in the heating season and zone 2 in conditioning (2026-11-18): a setpoint sent to zone 2 through the central unit (`*#4*#2*#14*0210*3##`) was then acknowledged by both an F461 and an F455 and ignored, while the direct `*#4*2*#14*0210*3##` set the thermostat. Nothing on the bus lists the zones of a central unit, so a zone discovered from the bus still follows the plant (#697), and the form of a setpoint write goes by its result:
    * `climate.set_temperature` on a zone written through the central unit returns as soon as the plant confirms it: the central unit's answer for the zone (`*4*110*#3##`, `*#4*#3*14*0250*1##`, 0.55 s on the live 3550), or the zone's own report of the new setpoint. It waits up to 3 s for that;
    * without it the zone's setpoint is read (`*#4*Z*14##`). If it is still the old one, the direct form `*#4*Z*#14*TTTT*3##` is written. A zone that takes it is written directly from then on (attribute `standalone_detected: true`, kept across restarts) and its plain season frame (`*4*0*Z##` / `*4*1*Z##`) becomes the mode shown; a later report of the central unit for the zone puts it back on the central form;
    * only what the plant reports decides. The central unit's answer confirms a write when it carries the written setpoint (`*#4*#3*14*0250*1##`); after a mode frame alone (all an F455 shows, `*4*110*#3##`) the zone is read at once, and a zone the central unit answered for is never written directly. A thermostat that is off still holds its setpoint, so reading the requested value back from it confirms nothing and the direct form is tried. When the gateway does not take that form and the zone holds the requested setpoint, the write has arrived and no error is raised (an F454 refuses the direct form for a zone of its central unit, #697). When it does not take that form and the zone still reports the old setpoint, the service call fails with the error of the write through the central unit, the one that was asked for. A write through the central unit that the gateway itself refused (NACK) fails the service call and nothing else is sent; a write with no reply in time is logged, since it may still have arrived. The value compared is the one on the wire (0.5 °C steps, 5.0 to 40.0 °C);
    * a zone written directly (`standalone: true`, the gateway option, or found from the bus) is told of a refused write as well. One exception: for a zone that was only found standalone from the bus, a refusal of the direct form drops that finding, and the same setpoint is written through the central unit and checked as above, without the direct form being tried a second time. On the F454 plant of #697 a zone of the central unit held standalone by mistake would otherwise have every setpoint refused; a thermostat that really is standalone gets an error for that one write and is found again by the next. `off`, `heat` and `cool` on such a zone fall back the same way (`*4*303*3##` was refused there, `*4*303*#3##` taken). The emulated firmware of F453, F453AV, H4684, MH200 and MH200N refuses the direct form `*#4*Z*#14*TTTT*M##` for every zone (own-firmware-oracle `trace-corpus.tsv:55`); this has not been seen on a real gateway of those models yet;
    * without any write, from the season: a zone found on the bus that reports the season the central unit is not in (`*4*0*Z##` while the central unit reports heating, such as `*4*102*#0##`, or the reverse) has its setpoint in force read again ten seconds later (`*#4*Z*12##`, which the thermostat answers with its season frame; live on an F461, an F455 and a MyHomeServer1). If it still reports that season and the central unit has not reported the zone meanwhile, it is handled as a standalone thermostat from then on (same attribute), so it shows its own mode (`cool` next to a central unit in heating) instead of the central unit's. Ten seconds because a zone of a central unit follows a season change within about two seconds (#453). After the central unit itself changes season no zone is compared with it for a minute: its zones follow one after the other ("within about 14 seconds" on the F454 plant with a 99-zone central unit of #697), and a zone read before its turn still answers in the old season. A standalone thermostat in the same season as the central unit cannot be told apart this way and is found by its first setpoint write;
    * a zone that takes neither form, or a `myhome.yaml` zone (`standalone: false` is the default there) that does not take the central form, makes the service call fail with an error instead of a dial that jumps back. A zone that answers nothing at all is only logged.

    To set it by hand, list the zone under **Standalone Thermostats** in the gateway options (*Settings → Devices & Services → MyHOME → Configure*), or set `standalone: true` on the zone in `myhome.yaml`.
* **Modes offered**: on a plant that also has a central unit, a standalone thermostat does not offer `auto` once the central unit is known (the central unit has no program for it: the live F461 ACKs `*4*311*#2##` and nothing answers; `auto` on zone 2 was refused on both gateway entries, 2026-11-12). A zone *of* the central unit does not offer the off-season mode while the season is reported (`heat` on zone 4 of a 3550 in conditioning was refused, 2026-11-12). The mode a zone shows is never dropped from the list.
* **`cool` on a zone found on the bus**: `myhome.yaml` says whether a zone cools (`cool:`, `false` unless stated). A zone found on the bus has no such entry and learns it from the bus: it offers `cool` once its valve status shows an actuator on the conditioning side (`*#4*<zone>*19*CV*HV##` with `CV` not `0`: a cooling fan coil reports its state there even when it is off, `*#4*47*19*5*0##`) or once the zone is reported in conditioning (its own `*4*0*<zone>##`, or the central unit's `*4*210*#<zone>##`). A heating-only zone reports `0` on that side (`*#4*49*19*0*0##`) and offers `off`, `auto` and `heat`, as it does with `cool: false`; until 2.0.1 every zone found on the bus offered `cool`, and choosing it on a floor-heating zone put its thermostat in conditioning. What was learned is kept across restarts (attribute `cooling_seen`); a state saved by 2.0.0 counts when the zone was saved in `cool` or with a cooling fan coil (`fan_coil_cooling`), and a zone saved in `heat` with neither learns it from the bus again. A zone that cools through a plain valve reads `0` while that valve is off, so it offers `cool` from the first time it cools or is put in conditioning at the thermostat; `cool: true` in `myhome.yaml` says it at once. With a central unit that is already in conditioning when Home Assistant starts, a zone offers `cool` once the central unit's report about that zone, or the zone's own frame, has arrived: on a 3550 plant that report followed the zone's start-up reads 25 to 47 s after the zone's first frame (measured in the heating season), and it comes again with every quarter-hour push. In the conditioning season a central unit also reports a heating-only zone it runs in conditioning, so such a zone offers `cool` then: the bus does not tell an idle cooling valve from none. A central unit found on the bus offers both seasons, as before; one configured in `myhome.yaml` follows its `cool:` entry.
* **How a central unit is recognised**: by a frame of the central unit itself (such as `*4*WHAT*#0##`) or by a seasonal status or a dimension the central unit sends for one of its zones (`*4*110*#Z##`, `*#4*#Z*14*TTTT*M##`). A report that comes through an interface (a WHERE ending in `#4#II`) is another bus's central unit: the zones of the gateway's own bus stay what they are, nothing is asked on that bus and its season is not taken. Likewise a zone behind an interface and the local zone with the same number are two zones: each takes only its own frames. An actuator status (dimension 20) is never one, and with WHERE `#0` it creates no entity and reaches none: an F461 2.0.11 on a plant with no central unit answered the all-zone actuator request `*#4*#0*20##` with `*#4*#0*20*1##` (#944), and its zones stay standalone (`*#4*Z*#14*TTTT*M##`).

## 🌡️ Push-Driven Secondary Probes (`WHERE ≥ 100`)

In plants with secondary temperature sensors (e.g. BTicino `3455` radio probes connected via `L4577` radio interfaces), sensor addresses have 3 digits (e.g. `WHERE = 105` corresponds to the first secondary probe of Zone 5).

* **Push-Driven Telemetry**: These battery-powered or radio sensors broadcast their temperature updates periodically on the SCS bus and actively reject direct poll requests.
* **v2 Quiet Listening**: The v2 climate engine recognizes secondary probe addresses, operates in receive-only mode, and suppresses redundant polling to prevent gateway NACKs.

> [!TIP]
> **Projecting External Temperatures onto the Bus**:
> To publish Home Assistant temperature sensors (e.g. Zigbee room thermometers, weather forecasts, or ESPHome probes) onto an SCS probe address for display on wall screens and thermostats, use the [Expose to Bus Guide](expose.md).

## 🎛️ Supported Features & HVAC Modes

MyHOME climate entities support standard Home Assistant climate controls:

* **HVAC Modes**:
    - `heat`: System active in heating mode.
    - `cool`: System active in cooling / air-conditioning mode.
    - `auto`: Following scheduled setpoint or central program.
    - `off`: System frost-protection or thermal shutdown.
* **Target Temperature**: Adjustable setpoint slider in 0.5 °C increments.
  The slider shows the setpoint the thermostat regulates to (dimension 12, knob offset included) whenever the zone has reported it; the commanded setpoint (dimension 14) is only shown until then. On an F461 the two can differ (21.0 commanded, 23.5 in force on a live capture), and the attribute `local_target_temperature` carries the same value. When the zone leaves `off`, the value reported while it was off is taken as the protection setpoint (#383), so the commanded setpoint is shown until the zone reports dimension 12 again; after a `set_temperature` call the next report, dimension 12 or 14, is shown. The value restored after a restart of Home Assistant already carries the saved `local_offset`, so the first dimension 13 report does not add it again.
* **Current Temperature**: Real-time ambient reading reported by the probe.
  On single-session or uncaptured SCS-only gateways whose firmware drops the general request `*#4*0##` (F450, F459, H4684, MH200, MH201), each local zone asks for its own temperature with `*#4*<WHERE>*0##` after the startup sweep, so a standalone thermostat is not `unknown` until it next reports (#185). F454, MH202, F460, F461 and MyHomeServer1 drop `*#4*0##` as well but answer the zone status request `*#4*<WHERE>##` themselves (the F454 supports up to 4 command sessions and answers in ~1.3 s on both central-unit #629 and standalone #866 plants, the MH202 answers in ~160-250 ms, #866, the F460 and F461 expand it into one request, and the MyHomeServer1 replies from its cache), so their local zones keep that poll. A zone behind an F422 bus interface (`WHERE = <zone>#4#<interface>`) is never polled unless the OWNd profile sets `supports_interface_zone_requests = True` (see below), so it shows its restored reading until it reports by itself. After a restart the last reading is shown with the attribute `current_temperature_restored: true` until the zone reports again.
* **Current Humidity**: Ambient relative humidity percentage for zones equipped with humidity sensors (e.g. Living Now thermostats or humidity probes).
* **Zones found at start**: where the gateway forwards the general request `*#4*0##` (F453, F453AV, MH200N), every thermostat answers it and its zone is created. Where the firmware drops that request, the startup sweep asks instead for the state of every actuator with `*#4*0#0*20##` (F450, F454, F455, F459, F460, F461, MyHomeServer1; live on the F455, the F461 and the MyHomeServer1, by the firmware oracle on the others) and, on an F461 and a MyHomeServer1, for the central unit's status with `*#4*#0##`. A fan coil's answer creates its zone at once. A plain valve may be a towel warmer with no thermostat (#785), so its zone is first asked for its temperature (`*#4*<zone>*0##`), one zone at a time once the bus is quiet: a thermostat answers and the zone is created, and an address with no thermostat is left alone. An F461 and a MyHomeServer1 refuse a read nothing answers after about 2 s, so every such address is asked there; an F455 holds it for about 9 s and the other gateways were not measured, so there the reads stop after two such addresses in a row and the remaining ones wait for their own report. These reads are sent only by the gateways that are asked for every actuator at start: where the general request is forwarded, every thermostat has already answered it. On every other gateway the central unit's status is not asked at start (an F455 holds a request nothing answers for about 9 s; the other models were not measured). There it is asked once, when the central unit's report about one of its zones shows that it is there to answer, and the answer creates its entity (live on an F455; every emulated firmware forwards the request). A zone with no actuator, a zone behind an interface and the zones of an H4684, MH200, MH201 or MH202 still appear when they first report (a temperature change, a key, the central unit's quarter-hour push). On 2.0.0 nothing was asked on those gateways, and a freshly added F461 showed no thermostat until one spoke.
* **HVAC Action and `actuators`**: a local zone asks for the state of its actuators (`*#4*<WHERE>*20##`, one `*#4*0#0*20##` for every local zone on an F455, an F461 or a MyHomeServer1; `*#4*#0*20##` was sent by 2.0.0 and is answered alike by the F461 and the MyHomeServer1, not by the F455) once the startup sweep is over and again after the event session reconnects, so `hvac_action` (`heating` / `cooling` / `idle`) is known right after start instead of after the first actuator switch. The attribute `actuators` lists each actuator as `on` or `off` (`{"1": "off", "2": "off"}`) and stays while the zone is off (every actuator `off`). That `off` is an assumption until the actuator reports it: an actuator reports when it changes, so one that was `on` when its zone was switched off and has not reported since is asked again once the zone runs. Live 2026-11-19 (F461 2.0.11 and F455 1.0.76): a fan coil whose zone was off for 1.2 s never stopped and sent no frame, and the zone stayed `idle` while it ran; with the request, after an off of 2.0 s, `*#4*0#0*20##` was answered `*#4*2#1*20*8##` 1.3 s after the zone's on and the zone showed `cooling` again (`tests/fixtures/traces/zone_short_off_2026_11_19`). When an explicit point actuator `Z#N` (`1 <= Z <= 99`, `1 <= N <= 9`) reports a proportional 0–10 V output code (`Dimension 20` values `100..200`, e.g. Sabiana 0–10 V fan coils or modulating mixing valves behind an `F459` 0–10 V actuator interface), `actuators` remains `"on"` (`101..200`) / `"off"` (`100` = `0%`) for backwards compatibility while `actuator_levels` exposes the percentage (`0..100`) per explicit `Z#N` proportional actuator (e.g. `{"3": 55}`, resetting to `0` when the actuator or zone turns off; zone-wide `Z#0` or bare `Z` frames never spread percentages) alongside an auto-discovered per-actuator `%` `sensor` entity attached to the same zone device (see [Sensors & Energy](sensors.md)). `actuator_levels` is excluded from Home Assistant's recorder (`_unrecorded_attributes`) so modulating ramp steps record numeric history only on the `sensor` entity. F453, F453AV, H4684, MH200, MH200N and MH202 drop the zone actuator request (firmware oracle), so their zones are not asked and wait for the actuators' own frames; the F455 refuses the whole-zone form at once but answers the per-actuator form `*#4*<WHERE>#<N>*20##` (live F455 1.0.76), so its thermo actuator switches and proportional output-level sensors ask `*#4*<WHERE>#<N>*20##` (while on non-refusing gateways, proportional output-level sensors also register `Z#N` with `watch_zone_actuators`, coalesced into `*#4*0#0*20##` on `F461` / `MyHomeServer1` or sent as `*#4*Z#N*20##` on `3488`, `F450`, `F454`, `F455`, `F459`, and `F460`); on a shared bus the gateway that serves WHO 4 (delegated secondary or warm standby) decides. Secondary probes (`WHERE >= 100`) are never asked. Probe-less `F430/2` / `F430/4` relays (such as bathroom towel warmers that only emit Dimension 20 actuator status frames without a temperature probe) do not create phantom `climate` entities and can be controlled as `switch` entities via **Add thermo actuator / towel-warmer switch (WHO 4)** (see [Switches & Relays](switches.md)).
* **Zones that have not reported after a start (every gateway)**: 30 s after the startup sweep, and after its own startup poll, a zone that has not reported its temperature, setpoint and mode (a value restored from before the restart does not count) asks for what is still missing, one read at a time: `*#4*<zone>*0##` (only when the temperature is missing), `*#4*<zone>*13##` (the knob, unless the zone already asked for it), `*#4*<zone>*14##`, `*#4*<zone>*12##` and `*#4*<zone>#1*20##` (not on F453, F453AV, H4684, MH200, MH200N and MH202, which drop the actuator request). A knob read that was sent when the zone was added and brought nothing is repeated once, as the last read. The zones are asked one after another; each read waits for the previous answer and then for the bus to be quiet (no frame for 0.75 s), and a refused or unanswered read (a NACK, a timeout, or an ACK that brought no frame) ends the poll, so a zone without a probe costs one read. A read that collected only another zone's frame is sent once more, unless the zone's own answer arrived in the meantime. This goes by the result, not by a list of gateway models: a zone that got everything from the sweep, from its status request `*#4*<WHERE>##` or from its thermostat sends nothing, so on a gateway that answers the status request (F454, F460, F461, MH202, MyHomeServer1) the poll normally asks nothing, and a zone that answered neither of its last two status requests (`unresponsive_zone` repair) is left alone. Until now only F455 zones were asked this way (#466); on the other gateways whose zones are not sent the status request, `temperature` stayed empty after a start until a thermostat published a change (#932 on an MH201). After a restart the saved setpoint is shown at once and is replaced by the zone's own value when it arrives. On a shared bus a passive standby or secondary asks nothing. Run on a physical F455 1.0.76 (#466, #932). On a physical MH201 3.6.43 the reporter of #932 ran the poll in its first form (the MH201 added to the F455's list) and every zone got its setpoint; for F450, F453, F453AV, F459, H4684, MH200 and MH200N the firmware oracle shows each of these reads going out to the bus, and the poll has not yet run on a physical plant; F452 and F458 are not in the oracle.
* **Zones on an F455 (Basic Gateway)**: the F455 ACKs `*#4*0##` only after about 9 s and sends nothing, so the startup sweep leaves that request out; it ACKs the zone status request `*#4*<WHERE>##` only after about 9 s and refuses `*#4*<WHERE>*20##` at once (live F455 1.0.76, `tests/fixtures/traces/issue_466`), so its zones ask nothing of their own when they are added (#674); the gateway sends one `*#4*0#0*20##`, the published request for all actuators, which the F455 answers with every actuator of the plant (live F455 1.0.76, 2026-11-18: five actuators within 0.4 s, ACK after about 1.4 s). Everything else, the knob included, is asked by the delayed poll above, each read answered in about 0.1 s (#466); a zone whose actuators that request did not report is asked for actuator 1 there. The actuator reports do not say which side of the zone a fan coil drives, so the poll ends with `*#4*<zone>*19##`: the state of both sides of the zone, whatever the number of actuators (a zone with a valve on actuator 1 and a fan coil on actuator 2 then shows `cooling` and its fan speed although actuator 1 is off). A repeated "off" of an actuator that was already off does not end what dimension 19 reported. The actuator numbers a zone reported are remembered across a restart, and the poll then asks for each of them instead of actuator 1 alone; an actuator the zone never reported is not asked (the F455 NACKs the read of an absent actuator only after about 9 s). A zone without a probe (an actuator-only channel) refuses the first read after about 9 s and is not asked anything else; that one read holds a command session for those 9 s, once per zone added or reloaded.
* **Zones Behind an `#4#II` Interface (`F422`)**: across all twelve emulated gateway firmwares in `own-firmware-oracle` (`findings/thermo-zone-requests.md`), **zero** gateways emit `$06EC`/`$06E4` interface-routed SCS telegrams for `WHO = 4`: gateways either NACK/drop `#4#II` queries, truncate `Z#4#II` at `#` and cross-poll local zone `Z` on the main bus (`F450`, `F454`, `F459`, `F460`, `F461`, `H4684`, `MH200`, `MyHomeServer1`), or on `MH202` (`1.0.24`) misparse trailing `#4#01` as local zone 4 actuator 1 and crash `coso` (`SIGSEGV`) on `*#4*Z#4#01##`. Unless an `OWNd` gateway profile explicitly declares `supports_interface_zone_requests = True`, zones configured with an `#4#II` interface are passive: neither polls (`*#4*Z#4#II##`, `*0##`, `*11##`, `*19##`, `*20##`, `*60##`) nor writes that would strip `#4#II` onto local main-bus zone `Z` are sent, while incoming event-session broadcasts update state normally (note that 1-digit inline shorthand `3#4#1`..`3#4#9` is normalized as zone `3` behind interface `01`..`09`; wire frames always use two-digit `#4#01`..`#4#15`). Dimensionless and zone-dimension polls on actuator or split addresses (`Z#N`, `Z#0`, `0#N`, `3#Z#N`) are likewise never sent.

### 💧 Humidity Support & Startup Behavior

Climate zones equipped with integrated humidity sensors (such as BTicino Living Now or specialized probes) report relative humidity via OpenWebNet `WHO = 4` Dimension `60` frames (`*#4*<WHERE>*60*<HUMIDITY>##`):

* **Dynamic Auto-Detection**: When a climate zone emits a humidity reading on the SCS bus, the integration dynamically enables humidity tracking on that entity.
* **State Restoration Across Restarts**: The zone remembers that it supports humidity across Home Assistant restarts and restores its last known humidity value immediately.
* **Targeted Startup Query**: After startup (once the initial discovery sweep finishes), only climate zones that support humidity are queried with `*#4*<WHERE>*60##` to refresh the current reading. This avoids unnecessary bus traffic on zones without humidity sensors. A live F461 and a live MyHomeServer1 answer the request (F459 and F460 forward it in the firmware emulation); on F450, F453, F453AV, F454, H4684, MH200, MH200N and MH202 the emulated firmware did not forward it (own-firmware-oracle `findings/thermo-zone-requests.md`, a `firmware_observed` verdict), so until the OWNd gateway profile states `supports_heating_humidity_request` their zones are not queried. Whether those gateways put a probe's humidity on the event session has not been traced; any `*#4*<WHERE>*60*<HH>##` event the gateway delivers still updates `current_humidity` without polling.
* **Optional YAML Configuration**: In advanced setups using legacy `/config/myhome.yaml`, humidity polling can be explicitly pre-enabled on a zone by setting `humidity: true` under the zone configuration.
