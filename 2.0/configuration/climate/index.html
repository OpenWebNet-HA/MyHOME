    Climate & Heating (WHO = 4)¶

    The MyHOME integration provides full control for heating, cooling, central thermoregulation units, and zone thermostats operating on OpenWebNet WHO = 4.

    In v2, setup and management are 100% UI-first: climate zones and central units are automatically discovered over the SCS bus without manual YAML configuration files.

    🚀 Auto-Discovery¶

    When your gateway connects to Home Assistant:

    1. Dynamic Bus Discovery: The integration listens to OpenWebNet WHO = 4 temperature and setpoint messages broadcast on the SCS bus.
    2. Candidate Confirmation (Bit-Flip & Phantom Zone Guard): An unconfigured bus zone (WHERE = 1..99) is held as a candidate on its first frame rather than creating a permanent entity from a single SCS telegram corruption (for example, a 1-bit flip turning Zone 1 *#4*1*0*0190## into phantom Zone 65 *#4*65*0*0190##). A local unrouted zone is confirmed either passively by a second independent frame within 1 hour (ignoring identical repeats within 5.0 s from gateways such as MyHomeServer1) or actively once the bus is quiet by querying *#4*Z*0##: a thermostat answers with its temperature and the zone is created. When nothing answers (a gateway NACK, or an ACK with no reply) the zone stays a candidate, is not queried again, and is created only if a second independent frame of it arrives within 1 hour of the first. Zones already in myhome.yaml or the entity registry, the 99-zone central unit (#0), and a 4-zone central unit (#0#N) are accepted immediately; zones behind an F422 interface (Z#4#II) are never actively queried and confirm passively on a second independent frame.
    3. Entity Creation: Each confirmed thermoregulation zone (WHERE = 1..99, 99-zone central unit #0, or 4-zone central unit #0#N) is registered as a Home Assistant climate entity linked to your gateway, replaying the held first frame so no initial state is lost.
    4. UI Customization: You can rename the zone, assign it to an Area (e.g. Master Bedroom, Living Room), and adjust temperature step increments directly in the Home Assistant UI.

    🏛️ Supported Plant Architectures¶

    MyHOME installations typically deploy one of three climate topologies, all natively supported by the v2 discovery engine:

    1. 99-Zone Central Unit Architecture (BTicino 3550 / #0)¶

    • Hardware: Central thermoregulation unit (BTicino 3550, MyHOME_Screen 10, or MyHOME_Screen 3.5) operating at master address WHERE = #0.
    • Subordinate Zones: Individual room zones (WHERE = 1..99) with local probes (L4693, H4693, LN4693, etc.) and F430/2 or F430/4 flush/DIN actuators.
    • Operation: The integration discovers the central unit (#0) as the master climate entity and each individual zone (1..99) as a controllable climate device. Subordinate zone setpoint and mode commands use the central-unit-routed #Z address format (*#4*#Z*#14*TTTT*M##, *4*311*#Z##, *4*303*#Z##).
    • Central unit modes: off sends *4*303*#0##; auto sends the published last-weekly-program command *4*3100*#0## (WHO 4 PDF §1.6.8); heat / cool send the season frame *4*1*#0## / *4*0*#0## (OWNd's set_central_mode builds the frames). History: OWNd up to 2.0.0b13 built *4*311*#0## for auto, which a live 3550 behind an F461 did not act on (ACKed and forwarded, central unit still OFF 8 s later, 2026-11-12); the same 3550 answered *4*3100*#0## within 0.15 s with *4*2101*#0## (weekly program 1, conditioning). off / auto wait at most 15 seconds for the gateway's answer to the frame: a refusal (NACK) within that time fails the call with an error and nothing is shown, on the central unit or on its zones. Otherwise the mode is shown as sent (and off on the zones too) and then follows the central unit's report: when the gateway took the frame, when nothing answered within the 15 seconds, and when the frame was dropped without a NACK (a lost connection, for one), so a mode shown this way is not a confirmation by the gateway; a refusal that comes after the 15 seconds no longer fails the call nor undoes what is shown. A report of the central unit that arrives during the wait stays what is shown (after a program restore the entity shows the reported season and program); heat / cool wait for the central unit's answer (the mode it is in, or nothing when the season does not change; live F461 + F455, 2026-11-08). On its own the season frame leaves a 3550 in that season's protection, or changes nothing when it is already in that season, in protection or off; so on the 99-zone central unit (#0), when the central unit has reported protection, off or the other season since Home Assistant started, the season frame is followed by *4*3100*#0## and the 3550 starts the last weekly program of that season (live F461 + F455, 2026-11-18). *4*3100*#0## goes out only after the season frame was written, and the call fails when the gateway refuses either frame, does not write it or does not confirm it (the wait for a frame to be written ends after 15 seconds, and the frame is then withdrawn); no other command of the entity goes out between the two. A central unit that reported running in that season, or that has not reported since Home Assistant started (after a restart only the saved state is known), gets the season frame only; a manual setpoint is set with climate.set_temperature.
    • Startup & Reconnect Status Burst (*#4*#0##): On startup (including on SCS-only gateways such as MH200N and F454), the central unit entity queries *#4*#0##. The 3550 responds with its operating mode frame (e.g. *4*1101*#0## for Heating Weekly Program 1, *4*110#0200*#0## for Manual 20.0 °C, or *4*102*#0## for Antifreeze/Protection) followed by diagnostic status frames (WHAT 20..24, 30, 31), exposed on the #0 entity as state attributes:
      • remote_control: "enabled" (*4*21*#0##) or "disabled" (*4*20*#0##, raises the self-clearing central_remote_control_disabled repair issue).
      • at_least_one_probe_off: true when *4*22*#0## is reported in the current burst.
      • at_least_one_probe_antifreeze: true when *4*23*#0## is reported in the current burst.
      • at_least_one_probe_manual: true when *4*24*#0## is reported in the current burst.
      • failure_detected: true when *4*30*#0## (Failure discovered in a zone) is reported in the current burst.
      • battery_fault: true when *4*31*#0## (Central unit battery KO) is reported in the current burst (raises the self-clearing central_battery_fault repair issue).
    • Remote Control (Telegestione) Requirement: On a physical BTicino 3550, Remote Control (Telegestione) must be set to Enabled (Abilitata) in the 3550 menu (*4*21*#0##) for the central unit to accept remote commands over the bus. When *4*20*#0## is active, the gateway still replies *#*1## (ACK), but the 3550 ignores remote mode and setpoint writes.
    • What the thermostat shows: the mode is always what the system is doing, heat or cool (or off), also while a weekly program or scenario runs; hvac_action shows whether the zone's actuators are heating, cooling or idle. auto is only a command ("go back to the program"): on the central unit it is shown until the central unit reports, and is then replaced by heat / cool; it is never passed on to the central unit's zones, which keep the mode they show until they or the central unit report a season. (Up to 2.0.0 every zone of the central unit that was not off was shown auto from the central unit's auto until its next report: 0.15 s on a live 3550, and for as long as no report followed.) The program is visible as program / scenario on the central unit and zone_operation: automatic on a zone.
    • Subordinate Zone Control Patterns (Auto vs Manual):
    Control Pattern Central Unit (#0) Mode Subordinate Zone (1..99) Mode How Setpoint & Schedule Changes Work
    1. 3550 as Schedule Master + HA Temporary Overrides on a weekly program 1..3 or scenario 1..16: shown heat / cool (the season it runs in) with program / scenario on the program (*4*111*#Z## / *4*211*#Z##): shown heat / cool with zone_operation: automatic Calling climate.set_temperature on a zone writes *#4*#Z*#14*TTTT*3##, placing that zone into manual setpoint override (*4*110*#Z##, hvac_mode: heat/cool, #0 reports at_least_one_probe_manual: true). Setting the zone's hvac_mode back to auto sends *4*311*#Z## and returns the zone to the 3550's active weekly schedule.
    2. Home Assistant as Full Schedule Master heat, cool, or auto heat / cool (Manual setpoint per room) Each zone is driven directly by Home Assistant automations or the HA Schedule integration calling climate.set_temperature (*#4*#Z*#14*TTTT*3##) or climate.set_hvac_mode (heat, cool, off) on each room entity independently.

    2. 4-Zone Central Unit Architecture (BTicino 4695 / #0#N)¶

    • Hardware: BTicino HC4695, L4695, LN4695, AM5875 4-zone master display panel configured at base zone N (1..96 on physical hardware; OpenWebNet gateways accept 1..99, reporting at #0#N).
    • Unified master entity (Central Unit 01..Central Unit 96): The 4695 master panel embeds zone N's probe and actuator control and drops direct zone writes (*#4*N*#14*..., *4*303*N##) at its own base zone in firmware (LH4695_AM5875_010103.fwz, ROM 0x0E987C..0x0E989C). The integration therefore creates a single unified Central Unit entity (zero-padded two-digit zone suffix, e.g. Central Unit 01 for #0#1, Central Unit 21 for #0#21) at #0#N that owns master zone N's telemetry (*#4*N*0*... ambient temperature, *#4*N*13*... local offset, *#4*N#A*20*... actuators, and *#4*N*12*... active schedule setpoint) and does not create a separate dead Climate Zone N entity — unless zone N is explicitly configured in myhome.yaml or listed in Standalone Thermostats in the gateway options.
    • Subordinate zones (N+1..N+3): Subordinate zones are discovered as ordinary climate entities and written directly (*#4*Z*#14*TTTT*3##, *4*303*Z##), the form answered on the F454 2.0.51 plant of #994 (*#4*3*#14*0170*3## set zone 3 alone while *#4*#3#1*#14*0175*3## got no answer, tests/fixtures/traces/issue_994/). A 4-zone central unit report does not set the 99-zone has_central_unit flag, so zones stay written directly unless a 99-zone central unit (#0 or #Z reports) is present. Because the 4695 has no per-zone return-to-program command (*4*311*#Z## is ignored), setting auto on a subordinate zone writes its current manual setpoint; to return the whole plant to the 4695 weekly schedule, set auto on the central unit (Central Unit 01..Central Unit 96, sending *4*3100*#0#N##).
    • Central unit commands (#0#N):
      • Setpoint: climate.set_temperature writes generic manual mode M = 3 (*#4*#0#N*#14*TTTT*3##, SCS C1 02, 5.0..35.0 °C per 4695 ROM 0x0EA350..0x0EA3AB), which keeps the active season on both the 4695 (ROM 0x0EA31F..0x0EA347, #994 frame 5) and the 3550 (#0, live F461 2026-11-23), and updates state strictly from the central unit's bus report (*4*110#TTTT*#0#N## / *4*210#TTTT*#0#N##). Writing M = 1 in cooling or M = 2 in heating is dropped by 4695 firmware.
      • Season & wake (heat / cool): When the requested mode is in the opposite season from what #0#N reported (or restored across a restart), the integration sends the season frame (*4*1*#0#N## / *4*0*#0#N##, which switches season into protection) followed by *4*3100*#0#N## to resume the active weekly program in the new season (matching #0, unless a live non-idle report in the target season arrived while the season command waited). In the same season (or when no season is known yet), the season frame is omitted (it is a no-op that Gen D gateways NACK): heat / cool write *#4*#0#N*#14*TTTT*3## when resuming a manual setpoint, or *4*3100*#0#N## when on a weekly program (1..3), carrying only a master-zone Dimension 12 schedule setpoint, or waking with no known setpoint (preserving the weekly schedule instead of overwriting it with a manual override). Cross-season heat / cool wait for the central unit's bus report (matching #0), while same-season heat / cool, off, and auto update the displayed mode once acknowledged unless a bus report arrived first, no season is known yet (for heat/cool), remote_control == "disabled" (*4*20*#0#N##), or delivery was unwritten/cancelled.
      • off / auto, polling & diagnostics: off sends *4*303*#0#N##; auto sends *4*3100*#0#N## (verified in 4695 ROM 0x0EA383 and own-firmware-oracle plant emulation across Gen B/C/D gateways). On startup and homeassistant.update_entity, #0#N polls *#4*#0#N## (answered in ~140 ms in #994 second trace frames 1..4; informational with track_health=False so an unanswered poll never raises an unresponsive-zone repair), and on gateways that drop *#4*0## also reads master zone N's ambient temperature (*#4*N*0##) when unified and not yet reported. Diagnostic status flags (*4*20/21*#0#N## remote control and *4*31*#0#N## backup battery fault) are tracked per central unit address #0#N.
    • myhome.yaml & gateway compatibility: zone: "1" (or any base zone "1".."96") with central: true configures #0#N; subordinate zones listed in myhome.yaml must set standalone: true (as in v0.9.3), because standalone: false is the YAML default for 99-zone plants. On Gen A gateways (MH200, H4684), #0#N commands (303, 3100, #14*T*1..3) are collapsed onto #0 (B1=00, ignored by 4695) while season frames (*4*1/0*#0#N##) and direct zone writes are NACKed (4695 is unsupported on Gen A); on Gen B gateways (MH200N, F453, F453AV), plant-wide #0#N commands work while individual direct zone writes (*#4*Z*#14*...) are NACKed by the gateway translator.

    3. Standalone Probes (No Central Unit)¶

    • Hardware: Autonomous room thermostats (BTicino H4691, LN4691, L4692) acting as independent temperature controllers without a master central display.
    • Operation: Each thermostat is exposed directly as an independent climate entity with full target setpoint and mode control.

    • Next to a central unit: a plant with a central unit can have a thermostat the central unit is not running. On the live test plant a 3550 wrote and reported zones 2, 3 and 4 while it was on its weekly program in conditioning (*4*211*#2##, 2026-11-12), and only zones 3 and 4 while it was in the heating season and zone 2 in conditioning (2026-11-18): a setpoint sent to zone 2 through the central unit (*#4*#2*#14*0210*3##) was then acknowledged by both an F461 and an F455 and ignored, while the direct *#4*2*#14*0210*3## set the thermostat. Nothing on the bus lists the zones of a central unit, so a zone discovered from the bus still follows the plant (#697), and the form of a setpoint write goes by its result:

      • climate.set_temperature on a zone written through the central unit returns as soon as the plant confirms it: the central unit's answer for the zone (*4*110*#3##, *#4*#3*14*0250*1##, 0.55 s on the live 3550), or the zone's own report of the new setpoint. It waits up to 3 s for that;
      • without it the zone's setpoint is read (*#4*Z*14##). If it is still the old one, the direct form *#4*Z*#14*TTTT*3## is written. A zone that takes it is written directly from then on (attribute standalone_detected: true, kept across restarts) and its plain season frame (*4*0*Z## / *4*1*Z##) becomes the mode shown; a later report of the central unit for the zone puts it back on the central form;
      • only what the plant reports decides. The central unit's answer confirms a write when it carries the written setpoint (*#4*#3*14*0250*1##); after a mode frame alone (all an F455 shows, *4*110*#3##) the zone is read at once, and a zone the central unit answered for is never written directly. A thermostat that is off still holds its setpoint, so reading the requested value back from it confirms nothing and the direct form is tried. When the gateway does not take that form and the zone holds the requested setpoint, the write has arrived and no error is raised (an F454 refuses the direct form for a zone of its central unit, #697). When it does not take that form and the zone still reports the old setpoint, the service call fails with the error of the write through the central unit, the one that was asked for. A write through the central unit that the gateway itself refused (NACK) fails the service call and nothing else is sent; a write with no reply in time is logged, since it may still have arrived. The value compared is the one on the wire (0.5 °C steps, 5.0 to 40.0 °C);
      • a zone written directly (standalone: true, the gateway option, or found from the bus) is told of a refused write as well. One exception: for a zone that was only found standalone from the bus, a refusal of the direct form drops that finding, and the same setpoint is written through the central unit and checked as above, without the direct form being tried a second time. On the F454 plant of #697 a zone of the central unit held standalone by mistake would otherwise have every setpoint refused; a thermostat that really is standalone gets an error for that one write and is found again by the next. off, heat and cool on such a zone fall back the same way (*4*303*3## was refused there, *4*303*#3## taken). The emulated firmware of F453, F453AV, H4684, MH200 and MH200N refuses the direct form *#4*Z*#14*TTTT*M## for every zone (own-firmware-oracle trace-corpus.tsv:55); this has not been seen on a real gateway of those models yet;
      • without any write, from the season: a zone found on the bus that reports the season the central unit is not in (*4*0*Z## while the central unit reports heating, such as *4*102*#0##, or the reverse) has its setpoint in force read again ten seconds later (*#4*Z*12##, which the thermostat answers with its season frame; live on an F461, an F455 and a MyHomeServer1). If it still reports that season and the central unit has not reported the zone meanwhile, it is handled as a standalone thermostat from then on (same attribute), so it shows its own mode (cool next to a central unit in heating) instead of the central unit's. Ten seconds because a zone of a central unit follows a season change within about two seconds (#453). After the central unit itself changes season no zone is compared with it for a minute: its zones follow one after the other ("within about 14 seconds" on the F454 plant with a 99-zone central unit of #697), and a zone read before its turn still answers in the old season. A standalone thermostat in the same season as the central unit cannot be told apart this way and is found by its first setpoint write;
      • a zone that takes neither form, or a myhome.yaml zone (standalone: false is the default there) that does not take the central form, makes the service call fail with an error instead of a dial that jumps back. A zone that answers nothing at all is only logged.

      To set it by hand, list the zone under Standalone Thermostats in the gateway options (Settings → Devices & Services → MyHOME → Configure), or set standalone: true on the zone in myhome.yaml. * Modes offered: on a plant that also has a central unit, a standalone thermostat does not offer auto once the central unit is known (the central unit has no program for it: the live F461 ACKs *4*311*#2## and nothing answers; auto on zone 2 was refused on both gateway entries, 2026-11-12). A zone of the central unit does not offer the off-season mode while the season is reported (heat on zone 4 of a 3550 in conditioning was refused, 2026-11-12). The mode a zone shows is never dropped from the list. * cool on a zone found on the bus: myhome.yaml says whether a zone cools (cool:, false unless stated). A zone found on the bus has no such entry and learns it from the bus: it offers cool once its valve status shows an actuator on the conditioning side (*#4*<zone>*19*CV*HV## with CV not 0: a cooling fan coil reports its state there even when it is off, *#4*47*19*5*0##) or once the zone is reported in conditioning (its own *4*0*<zone>##, or the central unit's *4*210*#<zone>##). A heating-only zone reports 0 on that side (*#4*49*19*0*0##) and offers off, auto and heat, as it does with cool: false; until 2.0.1 every zone found on the bus offered cool, and choosing it on a floor-heating zone put its thermostat in conditioning. What was learned is kept across restarts (attribute cooling_seen); a state saved by 2.0.0 counts when the zone was saved in cool or with a cooling fan coil (fan_coil_cooling), and a zone saved in heat with neither learns it from the bus again. A zone that cools through a plain valve reads 0 while that valve is off, so it offers cool from the first time it cools or is put in conditioning at the thermostat; cool: true in myhome.yaml says it at once. With a central unit that is already in conditioning when Home Assistant starts, a zone offers cool once the central unit's report about that zone, or the zone's own frame, has arrived: on a 3550 plant that report followed the zone's start-up reads 25 to 47 s after the zone's first frame (measured in the heating season), and it comes again with every quarter-hour push. In the conditioning season a central unit also reports a heating-only zone it runs in conditioning, so such a zone offers cool then: the bus does not tell an idle cooling valve from none. A central unit found on the bus offers both seasons, as before; one configured in myhome.yaml follows its cool: entry. * How a central unit is recognised: by a frame of the 99-zone central unit itself (such as *4*WHAT*#0##; a 4-zone central unit's *4*WHAT*#0#N## is not one, see section 2) or by a seasonal status or a dimension the central unit sends for one of its zones (*4*110*#Z##, *#4*#Z*14*TTTT*M##). A report that comes through an interface (a WHERE ending in #4#II) is another bus's central unit: the zones of the gateway's own bus stay what they are, nothing is asked on that bus and its season is not taken. Likewise a zone behind an interface and the local zone with the same number are two zones: each takes only its own frames. An actuator status (dimension 20) is never one, and with WHERE #0 it creates no entity and reaches none: an F461 2.0.11 on a plant with no central unit answered the all-zone actuator request *#4*#0*20## with *#4*#0*20*1## (#944), and its zones stay standalone (*#4*Z*#14*TTTT*M##).

    🌡️ Push-Driven Secondary Probes (WHERE ≥ 100)¶

    In plants with secondary temperature sensors (e.g. BTicino 3455 radio probes connected via L4577 radio interfaces), sensor addresses have 3 digits (e.g. WHERE = 105 corresponds to the first secondary probe of Zone 5).

    • Push-Driven Telemetry: These sensors broadcast their temperature on the SCS bus by themselves, so the integration does not ask them when they are added.
    • Fallback read: a probe that has sent nothing for 15 minutes is asked at the next periodic update (every 5 minutes): a slave probe (PZZ) on dimension 0, an external probe (N00) on dimension 15. A refusal stops the read until the gateway that served it reconnects or another gateway serves the read (a delegated secondary, the warm standby, the primary when it is back); a refused read is not asked again while no gateway is connected. A probe or a zone sensor behind an interface is not asked where the gateway does not route WHO 4.

    Tip

    Projecting External Temperatures onto the Bus: To publish Home Assistant temperature sensors (e.g. Zigbee room thermometers, weather forecasts, or ESPHome probes) onto an SCS probe address for display on wall screens and thermostats, use the Expose to Bus Guide.

    🎛️ Supported Features & HVAC Modes¶

    MyHOME climate entities support standard Home Assistant climate controls:

    • HVAC Modes:
      • heat: System active in heating mode.
      • cool: System active in cooling / air-conditioning mode.
      • auto: a command, not a state: "go back to the program" (the central unit's last weekly program, or one of its zones back on that program). What follows is shown as heat / cool, see "What the thermostat shows" above.
      • off: System frost-protection or thermal shutdown.
    • zone_operation (zones, state attribute): what the zone's mode frame says besides its season. antifreeze (*4*102*Z##) and thermal_protection (*4*202*Z##): the zone is shown off at its protection setpoint (7.0 / 35.0 °C on a live 3550 plant, #383) and still heats below / cools above it, unlike a zone that is switched off (*4*303*Z##, no zone_operation). automatic (*4*111*#Z## / *4*211*#Z##, the central unit's program) and manual (*4*110*#Z## / *4*210*#Z##): both are shown heat / cool, the season they run in. protection is the generic *4*302*Z##. The central unit has central_operation and program instead.
    • Target Temperature: Adjustable setpoint slider in 0.5 °C increments. The slider shows the setpoint the thermostat regulates to (dimension 12, knob offset included) whenever the zone has reported it; the commanded setpoint (dimension 14) is only shown until then. On an F461 the two can differ (21.0 commanded, 23.5 in force on a live capture), and the attribute local_target_temperature carries the same value. When the zone leaves off, the value reported while it was off is taken as the protection setpoint (#383), so the commanded setpoint is shown until the zone reports dimension 12 again; after a set_temperature call the next report, dimension 12 or 14, is shown. The value restored after a restart of Home Assistant already carries the saved local_offset, so the first dimension 13 report does not add it again. The attribute nominal_temperature is the commanded setpoint without the knob offset: while a zone is off (off, antifreeze or thermal protection) the slider shows the protection setpoint (7.0 on a zone in antifreeze, #383) and nominal_temperature the setpoint the zone goes back to. It is saved across a restart, so heat / cool on a zone that was off or in protection when Home Assistant stopped write that setpoint and not the protection one; a state saved by an earlier version has none: heat / cool then fail at once with the error of the next paragraph and send nothing, until a temperature is set (climate.set_temperature works at once) or the zone reports its dimension 14. A zone seen off or in protection that has not reported a setpoint yet answers heat / cool (and auto, on a standalone zone of a plant without a central unit) with an error asking to set a temperature first, and a command the gateway refuses is reported as an error: a zone command written through the central unit (off, auto, heat / cool) and a fan speed; the central unit's own off and auto are covered under "Central unit modes" above.
    • Current Temperature: Real-time ambient reading reported by the probe. On single-session or uncaptured SCS-only gateways whose firmware drops the general request *#4*0## (F450, F459, H4684, MH200, MH201), each local zone asks for its own temperature with *#4*<WHERE>*0## after the startup sweep, so a standalone thermostat is not unknown until it next reports (#185). F454, MH202, F460, F461 and MyHomeServer1 drop *#4*0## as well but answer the zone status request *#4*<WHERE>## themselves (the F454 supports up to 4 command sessions and answers in ~1.3 s on both central-unit #629 and standalone #866 plants, the MH202 answers in ~160-250 ms, #866, the F460, the F461 and the MyHomeServer1 expand it onto the bus), so their local zones keep that poll. A zone behind an F422 bus interface (WHERE = <zone>#4#<interface>) is never polled unless the OWNd profile sets supports_interface_zone_requests = True (see below), so it shows its restored reading until it reports by itself. After a restart the last reading is shown with the attribute current_temperature_restored: true until the zone reports again.
    • Current Humidity: Ambient relative humidity percentage for zones equipped with humidity sensors (e.g. Living Now thermostats or humidity probes).
    • Companion Temperature & Humidity Sensors (sensor.*): Every confirmed non-central WHO = 4 climate zone automatically exposes a strictly passive (should_poll = False) companion Temperature sensor (device_class: temperature, state_class: measurement, native unit °C, enabled by default) attached to the same Home Assistant device for Long-Term Statistics, dashboards, and automations, and dynamically creates a companion Humidity sensor (device_class: humidity, state_class: measurement, unit %) only when the zone reports Dimension 60 (*#4*<WHERE>*60*<HH>##), restores humidity state, or declares humidity: true in myhome.yaml. Neither companion sensor sends bus polls (*#4*Z*0## / *#4*Z*60## polling stays exclusively on climate), and central units (#0, #0#1..#0#96), probe-less thermo switches (#785), exposed virtual probes, ignored addresses, and unconfirmed zones never create companion sensors (see Sensors & Energy).
    • Zones found at start: where the gateway forwards the general request *#4*0## (F453, F453AV, MH200N), every thermostat answers it and its zone is created. Where the firmware drops that request, the startup sweep asks instead for the state of every actuator with *#4*0#0*20## (F450, F454, F455, F459, F460, F461, MyHomeServer1; live on the F455, the F461 and the MyHomeServer1, by the firmware oracle on the others) and, on an F461 and a MyHomeServer1, for the central unit's status with *#4*#0##. A fan coil's answer creates its zone at once. A plain valve may be a towel warmer with no thermostat (#785), so its zone is first asked for its temperature (*#4*<zone>*0##), one zone at a time once the bus is quiet: a thermostat answers and the zone is created, and an address with no thermostat is left alone. An F461 and a MyHomeServer1 refuse a read nothing answers after about 2 s, so every such address is asked there; an F455 holds it for about 9 s and the other gateways were not measured, so there the reads stop after two such addresses in a row and the remaining ones wait for their own report. These reads are sent only by the gateways that are asked for every actuator at start: where the general request is forwarded, every thermostat has already answered it. On every other gateway the central unit's status is not asked at start (an F455 holds a request nothing answers for about 9 s; the other models were not measured). There it is asked once, when the central unit's report about one of its zones shows that it is there to answer, and the answer creates its entity (live on an F455; every emulated firmware forwards the request). A zone with no actuator, a zone behind an interface and the zones of an H4684, MH200, MH201 or MH202 still appear from their own reports, once confirmed as described under Candidate Confirmation (a temperature change, a key, the central unit's quarter-hour push). On 2.0.0 nothing was asked on those gateways, and a freshly added F461 showed no thermostat until one spoke.
    • HVAC Action and actuators: a local zone asks for the state of its actuators (*#4*<WHERE>*20##, one *#4*0#0*20## for every local zone on an F455, an F461 or a MyHomeServer1; *#4*#0*20## was sent by 2.0.0 and is answered alike by the F461 and the MyHomeServer1, not by the F455) once the startup sweep is over and again after the event session reconnects, so hvac_action (heating / cooling / idle) is known right after start instead of after the first actuator switch. The attribute actuators lists each actuator as on or off ({"1": "off", "2": "off"}) and stays while the zone is off (every actuator off). That off is an assumption until the actuator reports it: an actuator reports when it changes, so one that was on when its zone was switched off and has not reported since is asked again once the zone runs. Live 2026-11-19 (F461 2.0.11 and F455 1.0.76): a fan coil whose zone was off for 1.2 s never stopped and sent no frame, and the zone stayed idle while it ran; with the request, after an off of 2.0 s, *#4*0#0*20## was answered *#4*2#1*20*8## 1.3 s after the zone's on and the zone showed cooling again (tests/fixtures/traces/zone_short_off_2026_11_19). When an explicit point actuator Z#N (1 <= Z <= 99, 1 <= N <= 9) reports a proportional 0–10 V output code (Dimension 20 values 100..200, e.g. Sabiana 0–10 V fan coils or modulating mixing valves behind an F459 0–10 V actuator interface), actuators remains "on" (101..200) / "off" (100 = 0%) for backwards compatibility while actuator_levels exposes the percentage (0..100) per explicit Z#N proportional actuator (e.g. {"3": 55}, resetting to 0 when the actuator or zone turns off; zone-wide Z#0 or bare Z frames never spread percentages) alongside an auto-discovered per-actuator % sensor entity attached to the same zone device (see Sensors & Energy). actuator_levels is excluded from Home Assistant's recorder (_unrecorded_attributes) so modulating ramp steps record numeric history only on the sensor entity. F453, F453AV, H4684, MH200, MH200N and MH202 drop the zone form of the request (firmware oracle), so their zones are not asked when they are added; H4684 and MH200 drop the actuator form *#4*<WHERE>#<N>*20## as well, the other four forward it (firmware oracle, emulated), so a thermo actuator switch and the silent-zone poll below still ask there; the F455 refuses the whole-zone form at once but answers the per-actuator form *#4*<WHERE>#<N>*20## (live F455 1.0.76), so its thermo actuator switches and proportional output-level sensors ask *#4*<WHERE>#<N>*20## (while on non-refusing gateways, proportional output-level sensors also register Z#N with watch_zone_actuators, coalesced into *#4*0#0*20## on F461 / MyHomeServer1 or sent as *#4*Z#N*20## on 3488, F450, F454, F455, F459, and F460); on a shared bus the gateway that serves WHO 4 (delegated secondary or warm standby) decides. Secondary probes (WHERE >= 100) are never asked. Probe-less F430/2 / F430/4 relays (such as bathroom towel warmers that only emit Dimension 20 actuator status frames without a temperature probe) do not create phantom climate entities and can be controlled as switch entities via Add thermo actuator / towel-warmer switch (WHO 4) (see Switches & Relays).
    • Zones that have not reported after a start (every gateway): 30 s after the startup sweep, and after its own startup poll, a zone that has not reported its temperature, setpoint and mode (a value restored from before the restart does not count) asks for what is still missing, one read at a time: *#4*<zone>*0## (only when the temperature is missing), *#4*<zone>*13## (the knob, unless the zone already asked for it), *#4*<zone>*14##, *#4*<zone>*12## and *#4*<zone>#1*20## (not on H4684 and MH200, which drop the actuator form of the request too). A knob read that was sent when the zone was added and brought nothing is repeated once, as the last read. The zones are asked one after another; each read waits for the previous answer and then for the bus to be quiet (no frame for 0.75 s), and a refused or unanswered read (a NACK, a timeout, or an ACK that brought no frame) ends the poll, so a zone without a probe costs one read. A read that collected only another zone's frame is sent once more, unless the zone's own answer arrived in the meantime. This goes by the result, not by a list of gateway models: a zone that got everything from the sweep, from its status request *#4*<WHERE>## or from its thermostat sends nothing, so on a gateway that answers the status request (F454, F460, F461, MH202, MyHomeServer1) the poll normally asks nothing, and a zone that answered neither of its last two status requests (unresponsive_zone repair) is left alone. Until now only F455 zones were asked this way (#466); on the other gateways whose zones are not sent the status request, temperature stayed empty after a start until a thermostat published a change (#932 on an MH201). After a restart the saved setpoint is shown at once and is replaced by the zone's own value when it arrives; a zone saved while off that says it is running before its saved state is read shows the commanded setpoint with the knob offset (nothing when none was saved), not the saved protection setpoint. The commanded setpoint that was saved is asked for (*#4*<zone>*14##) until the zone reports it or, while it runs, its setpoint in force. On a shared bus a passive standby or secondary asks nothing. Run on a physical F455 1.0.76 (#466, #932). On a physical MH201 3.6.43 the reporter of #932 ran the poll in its first form (the MH201 added to the F455's list) and every zone got its setpoint; for F450, F453, F453AV, F459, H4684, MH200 and MH200N the firmware oracle shows each of these reads going out to the bus, and the poll has not yet run on a physical plant; F452 and F458 are not in the oracle.
    • Zones on an F455 (Basic Gateway): the F455 ACKs *#4*0## only after about 9 s and sends nothing, so the startup sweep leaves that request out; it ACKs the zone status request *#4*<WHERE>## only after about 9 s and refuses *#4*<WHERE>*20## at once (live F455 1.0.76, tests/fixtures/traces/issue_466), so its zones ask nothing of their own when they are added (#674); the gateway sends one *#4*0#0*20##, the published request for all actuators, which the F455 answers with every actuator of the plant (live F455 1.0.76, 2026-11-18: five actuators within 0.4 s, ACK after about 1.4 s). Everything else, the knob included, is asked by the delayed poll above, each read answered in about 0.1 s (#466); a zone whose actuators that request did not report is asked for actuator 1 there. The actuator reports do not say which side of the zone a fan coil drives, so the poll ends with *#4*<zone>*19##: the state of both sides of the zone, whatever the number of actuators (a zone with a valve on actuator 1 and a fan coil on actuator 2 then shows cooling and its fan speed although actuator 1 is off). A repeated "off" of an actuator that was already off does not end what dimension 19 reported. The actuator numbers a zone reported are remembered across a restart, and the poll then asks for each of them instead of actuator 1 alone; an actuator the zone never reported is not asked (the read of an absent actuator was not measured; an F455 held other reads nothing answered for about 9 s). A zone without a probe (an actuator-only channel) refuses the first read after about 9 s and is not asked anything else; that one read holds a command session for those 9 s, once per zone added or reloaded.
    • Zones Behind an #4#II Interface (F422): across all twelve emulated gateway firmwares in own-firmware-oracle (findings/thermo-zone-requests.md), zero gateways emit $06EC/$06E4 interface-routed SCS telegrams for WHO = 4: gateways either NACK/drop #4#II queries, truncate Z#4#II at # and cross-poll local zone Z on the main bus (F450, F454, F459, F460, F461, H4684, MH200, MyHomeServer1), or on MH202 (1.0.24) misparse trailing #4#01 as local zone 4 actuator 1 and crash coso (SIGSEGV) on *#4*Z#4#01##. Unless an OWNd gateway profile explicitly declares supports_interface_zone_requests = True, zones configured with an #4#II interface are passive: neither polls (*#4*Z#4#II##, *0##, *11##, *19##, *20##, *60##) nor writes that would strip #4#II onto local main-bus zone Z are sent (the writes are refused on every gateway, also one whose profile routes the requests: no WHO = 4 write names an interface), while incoming event-session broadcasts update state normally (note that 1-digit inline shorthand 3#4#1..3#4#9 is normalized as zone 3 behind interface 01..09; wire frames always use two-digit #4#01..#4#15). Dimensionless and zone-dimension polls on actuator or split addresses (Z#N, Z#0, 0#N, 3#Z#N) are likewise never sent.

    💧 Humidity Support & Startup Behavior¶

    Climate zones equipped with integrated humidity sensors (such as BTicino Living Now or specialized probes) report relative humidity via OpenWebNet WHO = 4 Dimension 60 frames (*#4*<WHERE>*60*<HUMIDITY>##):

    • Dynamic Auto-Detection: When a climate zone emits a humidity reading on the SCS bus, the integration dynamically enables humidity tracking on that entity and attaches a passive companion sensor.<zone>_humidity entity (%, state_class: measurement) to the same zone device for Long-Term Statistics.
    • State Restoration Across Restarts: The zone remembers that it supports humidity across Home Assistant restarts and restores its last known humidity value immediately.
    • Targeted Startup Query: After startup (once the initial discovery sweep finishes), only climate zones that support humidity are queried with *#4*<WHERE>*60## to refresh the current reading. This avoids unnecessary bus traffic on zones without humidity sensors. A live F461 and a live MyHomeServer1 answer the request (F459 and F460 forward it in the firmware emulation); on F450, F453, F453AV, F454, H4684, MH200, MH200N and MH202 the emulated firmware did not forward it (own-firmware-oracle findings/thermo-zone-requests.md, a firmware_observed verdict), so until the OWNd gateway profile states supports_heating_humidity_request their zones are not queried. Whether those gateways put a probe's humidity on the event session has not been traced; any *#4*<WHERE>*60*<HH>## event the gateway delivers still updates current_humidity without polling.
    • Optional YAML Configuration: In advanced setups using legacy /config/myhome.yaml, humidity polling can be explicitly pre-enabled on a zone by setting humidity: true under the zone configuration.
